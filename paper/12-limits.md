# What it does not do, and what would show it wrong

Escapement is a design. Its Bitcoin pieces run on regtest, its dynamics in simulation; nothing has
been deployed or tested against a real adversary. Its limits are of three kinds.

## What it does not do

It does not align an agent's goals. It records what an agent committed to and prices the record;
an agent that behaves well where it is recorded and differently elsewhere is the failure a record
cannot see. Standing built from forecasting edge rewards capability, which a misaligned agent has
as much as an aligned one, and for an agent that does not forecast it reduces to age, bond and a
count of breaches. It does not bind a model: the record belongs to a key, which can be
sold, and tying it to a model needs attestation from a lab or a hardware vendor. It does not make the
scorers neutral: whoever writes the event list, the baseline providers, the resolvers and the adjudicators are trusted parties, and capturing one of them captures standing. It does not stop an actor with its own
resources and counterparties, and on present evidence it does not stop a harmful agent from
re-entering under a fresh identity. It does not deter gains larger than an identity's stake and
standing, or aims that are not monetary. It does not make the guardians safe to have: any one can
end an identity, and together they can write its record. It does not bind a chain to its bond:
until that is specified and built, one bond can back many chains and two relying parties with
different pins can be shown different histories. Its rate limits, caps and tiers apply per chain, so
an operator can split an action across identities, entries and relying parties, and the agent labels
each request's tier itself. A promise can be written as a claim to avoid a stake, forecasts other
identities disclosed can be copied unless the baseline already includes them, and the agent's fee
bid at a veto's edge is not bounded by what its chain holds. And it does not give a clock for
chips.

## Where its guarantees are conditional

The fake-record bound holds only under H0: a baseline that is the conditional law of each outcome
given everything public when a round opens, published by an independent party, and a faker that uses
only public information and cannot move outcomes. A market baseline holds only up to the cost of
moving the market, and nobody can check that a real baseline meets H0. The price of a fake assumes
one chain per bond and a bond held for a year. The training claim holds only for events the agent cannot
influence, which nothing in the scheme can check. Adoption tips only when the gain from reading records, relative to the highest reading cost, is more than eight times the cost of joining, and some relying parties may never read. A
veto is as final as a payment of the same depth, so Tier 2 vetoes do not hold against a bribed large
pool. The cost of running the clock fast binds only actors below a hashrate majority, until the
timewarp fix is active. And the poor and the new start behind, because bonds cost money and records
take time.

## What would show it wrong

Each claim names a test. Measure the calibration of a candidate real baseline, such as a liquid
prediction market, and how fast identities using only public information gain round-rule score
against it: if they gain faster than K/λ allows, that baseline fails H0 and the bound does not apply
to it. If a chain bound to its bond as section 3 proposes still lets one bond back two histories, the
record's central property fails.
If an agent trained on complete records of exogenous events is no better calibrated, or no harder to
game, than one trained on records a lab curated, the training claim adds nothing. If relying parties that are offered agent records, with insurers reading them, do not price them, the adoption argument fails at
its first step; if harmful actors under an amortised identity cost still profit by cycling
identities in a model where agents plan ahead, the withdrawn routing-around claim stays withdrawn.
And if a tick-chain release can be counted by a relying party that follows its rules after a veto
confirmed at depth, or a lease release counted beyond h* + L on the honest chain, the enforcement
layer is broken where the lab found it sound.
